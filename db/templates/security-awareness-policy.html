Security Awareness Training Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

Most successful attacks on small businesses begin by tricking a person: a convincing email, a fake invoice, a phone call pretending to be IT, or an MFA prompt approved by mistake. This policy establishes a security awareness program so that everyone at {{client_name}} can recognize and report threats, understands their responsibilities, and practices safe habits.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, including owners and executives, as well as contractors, temporary staff and interns who have a {{client_name}} email account or access to company systems or data.

3. Roles and responsibilities

  • Security lead ([name, title]): owns the program, reviews completion and phishing reports, and follows up on overdue training.
  • {{company_name}}: administers the training and phishing simulation platform ([platform name]), enrolls and removes users, schedules campaigns, and provides monthly reports.
  • Managers: ensure their team completes training on time and support a culture where reporting is encouraged.
  • HR / office manager: notifies {{company_name}} of new hires and role changes so training is assigned, and keeps acknowledgment records.
  • All workforce members: complete assigned training by the due date, apply what they learn, and report suspicious activity.

4. Training requirements

  • Onboarding: new workforce members complete initial security awareness training and acknowledge the Information Security and Acceptable Use policies within 30 days of start, and before being granted access to Restricted data.
  • Annual training: all workforce members complete a comprehensive refresher at least once every 12 months, due by [month] each year.
  • Ongoing micro-training: short monthly training modules or security tips (5–10 minutes) delivered by the training platform.
  • Regulation-specific training: staff handling PHI complete HIPAA privacy and security training; staff handling payment cards complete PCI awareness; staff handling CUI complete CUI handling and insider threat awareness training — at onboarding and annually.
  • Event-driven training: additional communications or training when new threats emerge, after a security incident, or after significant changes to systems or policies.

5. Role-based training

  • Administrators and IT staff (anyone with privileged access): annual training on privileged account use, secure configuration, recognizing attacks targeting admins, and incident response responsibilities.
  • Finance, accounting, payroll and anyone who can move money or change payment details: annual training on business email compromise, invoice and vendor payment fraud, payroll diversion, gift card scams and the call-back verification procedure in section 7.
  • Executives and their assistants: training on targeted phishing ("whaling"), impersonation and deepfake voice/video fraud.
  • Front desk and customer service: training on phone-based social engineering, identity verification of callers, and handling of customer information.

6. Phishing simulations

  • {{company_name}} sends simulated phishing emails to all users at least monthly, varying templates and difficulty, including credential-harvesting, attachment, QR code ("quishing") and business email compromise scenarios.
  • Simulations are designed to teach, not embarrass. Results are reported in aggregate to management; individual results are shared only with the user, their manager and the security lead.
  • Users should report simulated phishing the same way they report real phishing, using the [Report Phishing] button. Reporting is tracked and recognized.
  • Program targets: click rate below 5% and report rate above 60% within 12 months, measured on a rolling 3-month average.

7. Required topics

Training content over each 12-month cycle covers at minimum:

  • Phishing and social engineering: recognizing suspicious emails, texts (smishing), phone calls (vishing), QR codes and social media messages; checking sender addresses and link destinations.
  • AI-generated phishing and deepfakes: attackers use AI to write flawless, personalized messages and to clone voices or video of executives and vendors. Good grammar and a familiar voice are no longer signs a request is genuine.
  • Wire fraud and call-back verification: any request to send money, buy gift cards, change bank or payroll details, or pay a new vendor must be verified by calling the requester at a phone number already on file or independently looked up, never a number provided in the email, text or voicemail making the request. Payments over [$ amount] require approval by a second person.
  • MFA fatigue and MFA code theft: never approve an MFA prompt you did not start; deny and report repeated prompts; never share MFA codes with anyone, including callers claiming to be {{company_name}} or a bank.
  • Passwords and password managers: long unique passphrases, using the company password manager, and what to do if a password is exposed.
  • Fake IT support and remote access scams: never install remote access software or allow a remote session for an unsolicited caller or pop-up; verify {{company_name}} by calling {{company_phone}}.
  • Safe handling of data: data classification, sending sensitive data securely, avoiding misdirected emails, and not using personal email or unapproved cloud storage or AI tools for company data.
  • Using AI tools safely: only approved AI tools may be used with company data; do not paste Confidential or Restricted data into public AI services.
  • Device and physical security: screen locking, protecting laptops and phones, tailgating, visitors, clean desk and secure printing.
  • Remote work and public Wi-Fi.
  • Malware and ransomware: suspicious attachments, macros, downloads and USB devices.
  • Incident reporting: how and when to report (section 8).
  • Insider threat awareness and recognizing unusual behavior (required where CUI is handled).
  • Applicable regulations and company policies.

8. Reporting

  • Report suspicious emails using the [Report Phishing] button in Outlook, or forward them to [address].
  • Report anything else suspicious, including if you clicked a link, entered a password, approved an unexpected MFA prompt, sent data to the wrong person, or lost a device, immediately to {{company_name}} at {{company_phone}} and to [security lead name].
  • {{client_name}} will not discipline anyone for promptly reporting a mistake. Delayed reporting makes incidents much worse.

9. Remedial training

  • A user who fails a phishing simulation (clicks a link, opens an attachment or enters credentials) is automatically assigned a short remedial module to complete within 7 days.
  • Users who fail 3 or more simulations within 12 months receive one-on-one coaching from {{company_name}} or the security lead, and their manager is informed.
  • Repeated failures after coaching may result in additional technical controls on the user's account (such as stricter email filtering) and are handled by management under the Enforcement section.

10. Tracking completion

  • {{company_name}} tracks training assignments, completion dates, phishing results and policy acknowledgments in the training platform and provides a monthly report to the security lead.
  • Overdue training triggers reminders at 7 and 14 days past due, then escalation to the manager. Training overdue by more than 30 days may result in suspension of system access until completed, as approved by the security lead.
  • Target: 100% of active workforce members current on required training; at least 95% at any time.
  • Training records are retained for at least [3] years, or 6 years where HIPAA applies, and are available for audits and cyber insurance applications.

11. Exceptions

Workforce members without company email or system access (for example some field or warehouse staff) may receive an alternative in-person briefing covering physical security, social engineering and reporting, documented with a sign-in sheet. Other exceptions require security lead approval.

12. Enforcement

Failure to complete required training or repeated disregard of security practices may result in loss of system access and disciplinary action up to and including termination.
