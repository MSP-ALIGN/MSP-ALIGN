Logging & Monitoring Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

This policy ensures {{client_name}} records the security-relevant activity needed to detect attacks, investigate incidents and demonstrate accountability, and that those records are monitored, protected and kept for an appropriate time. Many breaches go unnoticed for weeks because logs were never collected or reviewed.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all systems that store, process or protect company information, including identity systems (Entra ID, Active Directory), Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, workstations and servers, firewalls and VPN/ZTNA, endpoint detection and response (EDR), email security, backup systems, line-of-business applications that produce audit logs, and {{company_name}}'s remote management and support tools.

3. Roles and responsibilities

  • Security lead ([name, title]): owns this policy, receives escalations and decides on business actions during incidents.
  • {{company_name}}: configures logging, operates or contracts the centralized log platform and the managed detection and response (MDR) / security operations center (SOC) service, triages alerts, and reports monthly.
  • MDR/SOC provider ([provider name]): monitors security telemetry 24/7, investigates alerts, takes approved containment actions and escalates to {{company_name}}.
  • System owners: ensure line-of-business applications have audit logging enabled and tell {{company_name}} about new systems that should be logged.

4. Events that must be logged

Where the system supports it, the following events are logged with date/time, user or account, source (device name or IP address), the system or data affected, event type and outcome (success or failure), so that every action can be traced to the individual who performed it:

  • Authentication: successful and failed sign-ins, MFA challenges and failures, account lockouts, sign-ins from new locations or risky sign-ins, VPN connections.
  • Account and privilege changes: account creation, deletion, enable/disable; group and role membership changes (especially admin roles); password and MFA method resets.
  • Administrative actions: configuration changes to servers, firewalls, cloud tenants, conditional access, mail flow rules, and security tools; use of privileged and break-glass accounts; remote support sessions.
  • Security events: malware and EDR detections (anti-malware logs are kept for the same period as other security logs), blocked connections and intrusion prevention events, email threat detections, data loss prevention alerts, audit log clearing, starting, stopping or pausing of logging, access to the audit logs themselves, security tool disabled or tampered with.
  • System objects: creation and deletion of system-level objects such as services, scheduled tasks, databases and stored procedures on servers that hold Restricted data.
  • Network and command activity: DNS query logs and web (URL) request logs from the DNS filter, secure web gateway or firewall; network traffic flow logs from the firewall; DHCP logs (also used to find unknown devices for the asset inventory); and command-line and PowerShell activity on servers and workstations (captured by the EDR tool or Windows auditing).
  • Data access: access to, and export or sharing of, Restricted data where the application supports it (for example EHR audit logs, SharePoint/OneDrive sharing and bulk download events, mailbox access by non-owners, new inbox forwarding rules).
  • System events: service start/stop, backup success and failure, system errors affecting security, and capacity warnings (for example disk space, CPU or storage nearly full) that could affect availability.
  • Vendor and {{company_name}} activity: remote support sessions and administrative actions by {{company_name}} and other service providers.

Microsoft 365 unified audit logging and mailbox auditing are enabled for all users, with no account excluded from mailbox auditing. Windows advanced audit policy is configured per {{company_name}}'s baseline, including process creation events where the EDR does not already capture them. {{company_name}} reviews the list of logged events at least annually, and after incidents, and adds events that would have helped.

5. Centralized collection

  • Logs from the sources in section 2 are forwarded to a centralized log platform or SIEM ([platform name]) managed by {{company_name}} or its MDR partner, so logs survive the loss or compromise of the original system.
  • {{company_name}} maintains a list of log sources and confirms at least monthly that each source is still sending logs. A log source that stops reporting for more than 24 hours generates an alert.
  • Logs must not contain passwords, full payment card numbers or other secrets. Applications are configured to mask such data.
  • Logs are sent to the central platform promptly (near real time where supported), and log storage is sized for the retention in section 7 with an alert before it fills up.
  • The central platform can search, filter and summarize logs on demand and produce reports for investigations and audits without changing the original records.
  • Log sources in use: [list, e.g. identity system, email, firewall, EDR, servers, line-of-business application].

6. Time synchronization

  • All systems synchronize time to an authoritative source: domain controllers to [pool.ntp.org / time.nist.gov / firewall]; domain members to domain controllers; cloud services use provider time.
  • Time drift greater than 5 minutes is corrected, and log timestamps include time zone or use UTC so events can be correlated accurately.
  • Only administrators can change time settings or time sources, and changes to them are logged.

7. Retention

  • Security logs are retained for at least 1 year, with at least the most recent 90 days immediately available for search. Older logs may be archived to lower-cost storage if they can be restored within [2 business days].
  • PCI DSS: audit logs for the cardholder data environment are retained for at least 12 months with the most recent 3 months immediately available (PCI DSS 10.5.1).
  • HIPAA: HIPAA requires that security policies and documentation, including records of activity reviews, be retained for 6 years (164.316(b)(2)(i)). {{client_name}} retains [audit logs for systems containing ePHI for 6 years / records of log reviews and incident investigations for 6 years].
  • Logs subject to a legal hold or active investigation are preserved until released by the security lead.
  • Where the CCPA/CPRA applies, records of consumer privacy requests and how they were answered are kept for at least 24 months.
  • Default retention in some cloud services is short (for example 90 or 180 days in Microsoft 365 depending on license); {{company_name}} ensures logs are exported or licensing is sufficient to meet this section.

8. Monitoring and alert triage

  • EDR, identity and email security telemetry is monitored 24 hours a day, 7 days a week by the MDR/SOC.
  • Alerts are triaged by severity:
    • Critical (active ransomware, confirmed account takeover, admin compromise): containment starts within 15 minutes; {{company_name}} and the security lead are called immediately.
    • High (malware execution, suspicious admin activity, impossible travel with successful sign-in): investigated within 1 hour; client notified the same business day.
    • Medium: investigated within 1 business day.
    • Low/informational: reviewed in weekly or monthly reporting.
  • Security alerts from cloud services (for example Microsoft 365 alert policies and identity protection) are sent to a monitored mailbox or {{company_name}}'s ticketing system ([address or queue]), never to a single person's inbox.
  • Alerts are analyzed using information from several sources (identity, endpoint, email, firewall) and current threat intelligence from vendors and information sharing sources, to understand what happened, how far it reached and what it affects.
  • Pre-approved containment actions the MDR/SOC or {{company_name}} may take without further approval: isolating a device from the network, disabling a user account, revoking sessions, blocking a sender or IP address. Actions affecting servers or many users require a call to the security lead first, unless an attack is actively spreading.
  • Confirmed incidents are handled under the Incident Response Plan.
  • {{company_name}} reviews privileged account activity, firewall and VPN summaries, and log source health at least weekly, and tunes alert rules at least quarterly to reduce false positives.
  • Where PCI DSS applies, security event logs and logs of critical systems for in-scope systems are reviewed at least daily, by automated means; logs of other in-scope systems are reviewed [weekly / monthly] as set by a targeted risk analysis. Exceptions and anomalies found are investigated and resolved.
  • Where HIPAA applies, {{company_name}} or the system owner reviews records of activity in systems containing ePHI (sign-ins, access reports, security incident tracking reports) at least [monthly / quarterly], and records the review.
  • Monitoring is how {{client_name}} watches the activity of authorized users and detects unauthorized access to, use of or tampering with customer information and other Restricted data.

Failures of security controls

  • The failure of a critical security control (for example the firewall, EDR or anti-malware, logging, MFA, backup, or change detection) generates an alert. {{company_name}} restores the control promptly, finds and records the cause and how long it was down, checks whether anything happened while it was down, and takes steps to stop it recurring.
  • {{company_name}} checks that security controls continue to work as intended through this monitoring, monthly reporting and the quarterly review.

9. Intrusion detection and change detection

  • Host-based: the EDR tool runs on all workstations and servers and detects and alerts on suspicious activity.
  • Network: the firewall's intrusion detection/prevention (IDS/IPS) is enabled at the internet edge [and between internal network segments, including at the boundary of the cardholder data environment where one exists], with signatures kept current.
  • Change detection: where PCI DSS applies or where critical files must not change unexpectedly (for example system files, configuration files, payment application files and logs), file integrity monitoring or an equivalent change detection tool alerts on unauthorized changes, checked at least weekly. Where HIPAA applies, change detection or audit logs on systems containing ePHI help confirm that ePHI has not been altered or destroyed improperly.
  • Payment pages: where {{client_name}} hosts or controls a web page that takes card payments, the page's scripts and security headers are monitored for unauthorized changes at least weekly.
  • Public web applications: [website or portal] is protected by a web application firewall or equivalent service that detects and blocks common web attacks, where in scope for PCI DSS.

10. Log protection

  • Read access to logs is limited to {{company_name}} security staff, the MDR/SOC and the security lead, based on job need. Users whose activity is logged cannot alter or delete logs.
  • Centralized logs are stored in write-once or tamper-evident storage where available, encrypted in transit and at rest, and changes to existing logs generate an alert.
  • Clearing of logs or disabling of audit logging generates a high-severity alert.
  • Only a small number of named administrators ([names or roles]) can change logging settings or manage the log platform. Administrator accounts on systems generating logs are separate from accounts that administer the log platform where practical.
  • Logs and other records used as evidence in an investigation are exported with their original timestamps, stored with restricted access, and handled with a record of who collected them, when and from where, so their integrity can be shown. Actions taken during an investigation are recorded.

11. Reporting

{{company_name}} provides a monthly security report summarizing alerts by severity, incidents, notable trends and log source health, reviewed by {{vcio_name}} with {{client_name}} at quarterly business reviews.

12. Privacy

Monitoring is performed to protect company systems and data, as described in the Acceptable Use Policy. Log data is used for security, operations, and legal or HR investigations authorized by management.

13. Exceptions

Systems that cannot produce or forward logs must be documented with compensating controls and approved by the security lead.

14. Enforcement

Disabling logging or security agents, or tampering with logs, is prohibited and may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination.
