Business Associate Agreement (BAA) Checklist & Inventory

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

This is a checklist and tracking inventory, not a Business Associate Agreement. Use it to confirm that each vendor's BAA contains the terms required by 45 CFR 164.504(e) and 164.314(a), and to track which vendors have signed. Have agreements drafted or reviewed by legal counsel. This template should be reviewed and tailored and is not legal advice.

1. Purpose

HIPAA permits {{client_name}} to share protected health information (PHI) with a business associate only after obtaining satisfactory assurance, in a written contract, that the business associate will appropriately safeguard it (45 CFR 164.502(e), 164.308(b)). This document lists every vendor that creates, receives, maintains or transmits PHI on {{client_name}}'s behalf, records BAA status, and provides a checklist for reviewing each agreement.

2. Who is a business associate?

  • A person or organization, other than a workforce member, that performs functions or services for {{client_name}} involving PHI — for example claims processing, billing, IT support, data storage, transcription, legal, accounting, consulting or shredding.
  • Cloud service providers that store ePHI are business associates even if the data is encrypted and they do not hold the key (HHS cloud computing guidance).
  • Subcontractors of a business associate that handle PHI are also business associates; the business associate must obtain a BAA from them.
  • Usually not business associates: "mere conduits" that only transport data without routine access (e.g. the U.S. Postal Service, internet service providers); workforce members; other providers receiving PHI for treatment; janitorial staff with only incidental exposure.
  • If in doubt, treat the vendor as a business associate and ask counsel.

3. Required BAA content checklist (164.504(e), 164.314(a))

For each agreement, confirm that it:

  1. [ ] Establishes the permitted and required uses and disclosures of PHI by the business associate, and does not authorize uses or disclosures that would violate the Privacy Rule if done by {{client_name}} (except for the business associate's proper management and administration and data aggregation, if stated).
  2. [ ] Requires the business associate not to use or further disclose PHI other than as permitted by the contract or required by law.
  3. [ ] Requires appropriate safeguards and compliance with the HIPAA Security Rule (Subpart C of Part 164) for ePHI, to prevent uses or disclosures not provided for by the contract.
  4. [ ] Requires reporting to {{client_name}} of any use or disclosure not provided for by the contract, including breaches of unsecured PHI as required by 164.410, and any security incident of which it becomes aware.
  5. [ ] States a reporting timeframe for breaches and incidents (HIPAA's outer limit is 60 days from discovery; many organizations require [5–10] days) and what information must be provided.
  6. [ ] Requires the business associate to ensure that any subcontractors that create, receive, maintain or transmit PHI on its behalf agree in writing to the same restrictions, conditions and requirements (164.502(e)(1)(ii), 164.504(e)(2)(ii)(D)).
  7. [ ] Requires PHI in a designated record set to be made available for individuals' access (164.524).
  8. [ ] Requires PHI to be made available for amendment and amendments to be incorporated (164.526).
  9. [ ] Requires information to be made available for an accounting of disclosures (164.528).
  10. [ ] To the extent the business associate carries out any of {{client_name}}'s Privacy Rule obligations, requires compliance with those requirements.
  11. [ ] Requires the business associate's internal practices, books and records relating to PHI to be made available to the Secretary of HHS for determining compliance.
  12. [ ] At termination, requires return or destruction of all PHI (with no copies retained), or, if infeasible, extends the contract's protections and limits further uses and disclosures to the purposes that make return or destruction infeasible.
  13. [ ] Authorizes {{client_name}} to terminate the contract if the business associate violates a material term.

Recommended additional terms (not required by HIPAA, negotiate as appropriate): minimum necessary use; encryption standards; cooperation and cost responsibility for breach notification; indemnification; cyber insurance requirements; right to audit or receive a SOC 2 report; data location (e.g. U.S. only); offboarding timelines; 42 CFR Part 2 terms if substance use disorder treatment records are involved.

5. Onboarding a new vendor

  1. Before any PHI is shared, determine whether the vendor is a business associate (section 2).
  2. Obtain and review the BAA against the checklist in section 3; involve counsel for non-standard terms.
  3. Review the vendor's security (questionnaire, SOC 2 report or equivalent) with help from {{company_name}}.
  4. Sign the BAA, store it at [location], and add the vendor to the inventory.
  5. Grant only the minimum access needed.

6. Offboarding a vendor

  1. Remove the vendor's accounts and access; {{company_name}} confirms removal.
  2. Obtain written confirmation that PHI has been returned or destroyed, or document why that is infeasible.
  3. Mark the vendor inactive in the inventory and keep the BAA and termination records.

7. Review cadence and retention

  • The inventory is reviewed at least annually during the HIPAA risk analysis, and whenever a new system or vendor is introduced.
  • BAAs are re-reviewed when renewed, when the services change, or when HIPAA rules change.
  • BAAs and this inventory are retained for at least six years from the date created or last in effect (164.316(b)(2), 164.530(j)).
