1. Purpose and scope

This Written Information Security Program (WISP) describes the administrative, technical and physical safeguards {{client_name}} uses to protect customer information, as required by the FTC Safeguards Rule (16 CFR Part 314). It applies to all employees, contractors and service providers who access customer information, in any format, on any system or device.

Customer information means any record containing nonpublic personal information about a customer, whether in paper, electronic or other form, that is handled or maintained by or on behalf of {{client_name}}.

2. Qualified Individual

{{client_name}} designates [name, title] as the Qualified Individual responsible for overseeing, implementing and enforcing this program. Where {{company_name}} performs information security functions as a service provider, {{client_name}} retains responsibility for compliance and the Qualified Individual oversees {{company_name}}'s work.

Primary contact: {{client_contact}} · {{client_contact_email}} · {{client_phone}}

3. Risk assessment

{{client_name}} maintains a written risk assessment that identifies reasonably foreseeable internal and external risks to the security, confidentiality and integrity of customer information, evaluates the sufficiency of existing safeguards, and describes how identified risks will be mitigated or accepted. The risk assessment is reviewed at least annually and whenever there is a material change to operations or systems.

4. Safeguards

4.1 Access control

Access to customer information is limited to authorized users who need it to perform their duties. Each user has a unique account. Access is reviewed at least [quarterly] and removed promptly when a person leaves or changes roles.

4.2 Inventory and where customer information lives

{{client_name}} maintains an inventory of the data, devices, systems and facilities that store or process customer information, managed according to their importance and risk. The device inventory is maintained by {{company_name}}. Check each place below and record where customer information is kept:

  • ☐ Desktops and laptops (including local downloads folders)
  • ☐ Removable media (USB drives, external disks) and phones
  • ☐ Filing cabinets, desks and other paper records
  • ☐ Offsite storage (records storage, a home office, a storage unit)
  • ☐ Client or customer portals
  • ☐ Cloud email and file storage: [services]
  • ☐ Line-of-business applications and databases: [systems]
  • ☐ Backups, local and cloud

4.3 Encryption

Customer information is encrypted in transit over external networks and at rest on laptops, servers, backups and removable media. When customer information is sent as an encrypted or password-protected file, the password is sent by a different channel than the file (for example by phone or text message), as described in the Encryption Policy.

4.4 Multi-factor authentication

Multi-factor authentication is required for anyone accessing any information system that contains or connects to customer information, including email, remote access, line-of-business and accounting software, and cloud services.

4.5 Application security

Software used to process customer information is kept supported and patched. Third-party applications are evaluated for security before adoption. [State whether any applications are developed in-house.]

4.6 Data retention and disposal

Customer information is securely disposed of no later than two years after it was last used to provide a product or service, unless it is needed for business operations or retention is required by law. The retention policy is reviewed at least annually. Paper records are shredded; electronic media are wiped or physically destroyed.

4.7 Change management

Changes to systems that store or process customer information are planned, approved, documented and tested before implementation.

4.8 Monitoring and logging

User activity on systems containing customer information is logged and monitored to detect unauthorized access or use. Endpoints are protected with managed endpoint detection and response. Event logs on systems holding customer information are reviewed at least every [90 days], in addition to automated alerting, and the review is recorded.

4.9 Physical safeguards

  • Clean desk: customer information on paper or screens is never left unattended. Paper records are put away and locked at the end of the day, and screens lock when people step away.
  • Visitors: visitors sign in and are escorted in areas where customer information is kept or processed, and are not left alone with records or unlocked devices.
  • Rooms and cabinets holding customer information or servers are locked, with keys or access codes limited to the people who need them.

5. Testing and monitoring

The effectiveness of safeguards is tested or monitored regularly through [continuous monitoring / annual penetration testing and vulnerability scans at least every six months], and after any material change.

6. Training and personnel

All personnel complete security awareness training at onboarding and at least annually, including phishing awareness, password and MFA practices, and how to report incidents. Information security is managed by qualified personnel or service providers who maintain current knowledge of threats.

  • Every employee signs to confirm they received and read this WISP, at hire and whenever it changes significantly. Signed acknowledgments are kept by the Qualified Individual.
  • Employees who handle especially sensitive accounts or records, as decided by the Qualified Individual, also sign a confidentiality or non-disclosure agreement.
  • When someone leaves, their access is removed and company devices, keys and records are returned on or before their last day.

7. Service providers

Service providers with access to customer information are selected for their ability to protect it, are contractually required to maintain appropriate safeguards, and are assessed periodically. Current providers:

  • [Line-of-business or accounting software provider]
  • [Cloud email and file storage provider]
  • [Backup provider]

8. Incident response

{{client_name}} maintains a written Incident Response Plan that defines goals, internal processes, roles and decision authority, internal and external communications, remediation, documentation and post-incident review. Suspected incidents are reported immediately to the Qualified Individual and to {{company_name}} at {{company_emergency_phone}}.

During an incident, the Service Manager named in the Incident Response Plan (or [name, title]) is the only person who speaks for {{client_name}} to the media, law enforcement, customers and other businesses. Other staff refer those questions to that person. This doesn't limit reporting an incident to the Qualified Individual, {{company_name}}, the cyber insurer or legal counsel.

If a notification event involves the unencrypted information of 500 or more consumers, the FTC will be notified within 30 days of discovery, in addition to any notifications required by state law.

9. Annual report

The Qualified Individual reports in writing at least annually to [owner / board / senior officer] on the overall status of the program, compliance with the Safeguards Rule, and material matters including risk assessment results, risk decisions, testing results, security events and recommended changes.

10. Program review

This WISP is reviewed and updated at least annually and whenever there are material changes to business operations, systems, or the results of testing and risk assessments.
