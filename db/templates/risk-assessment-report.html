2. Purpose

This assessment identifies reasonably foreseeable threats to the confidentiality, integrity and availability of {{client_name}}'s information and systems, evaluates the likelihood and impact of those threats given current safeguards, and recommends actions to reduce risk to a level management accepts. It supports {{client_name}}'s Information Security Policy and applicable requirements such as the HIPAA risk analysis (164.308(a)(1)(ii)(A)), the FTC Safeguards Rule risk assessment (314.4(b)), CMMC RA.L2-3.11.1 and PCI DSS targeted risk analysis. This report reflects conditions at the time of assessment and does not by itself establish compliance with any regulation.

4. Methodology

The assessment follows a process based on NIST SP 800-30 (Guide for Conducting Risk Assessments), scaled for a small or mid-sized business:

  1. Information gathering: interviews with [names/roles]; review of existing policies and documentation; review of {{company_name}} RMM, EDR, backup and ticketing data.
  2. Technical review: [internal and external vulnerability scans, Microsoft 365 secure configuration review (Secure Score / CIS benchmark), firewall rule review, Active Directory review, backup verification, email security (SPF/DKIM/DMARC)].
  3. Control assessment: current safeguards compared against [CIS Controls v8 Implementation Group [1/2] / NIST CSF 2.0 / NIST SP 800-171 / HIPAA Security Rule].
  4. Risk analysis: each threat and vulnerability pair is rated for likelihood and impact using the scales in section 6.
  5. Recommendations and treatment: recommendations are prioritized and reviewed with management, who record a treatment decision for each risk.

6. Rating scales

6.1 Likelihood

  • Very likely: expected to occur within the next year; the threat is common for businesses like this and current controls would not stop it.
  • Likely: could reasonably occur within the next year; controls are partial.
  • Possible: could occur in the next few years; controls exist but have gaps.
  • Unlikely: not expected; strong controls are in place or the threat is uncommon.

6.2 Impact

  • Severe: business cannot operate for more than [3] days; breach of Restricted data requiring notification; losses over [$ amount]; serious regulatory, legal or reputational harm.
  • Significant: major disruption for up to [3] days; limited exposure of Confidential data; losses of [$ range].
  • Moderate: disruption of a department or non-critical system for up to a day; minor data exposure; losses of [$ range].
  • Minor: little disruption and no data exposure; losses under [$ amount].

6.3 Risk rating

The risk rating combines likelihood and impact:

  • Critical: very likely or likely with severe impact. Requires action or executive decision within 30 days.
  • High: very likely with significant impact, likely with significant impact, or possible with severe impact. Remediate within 90 days.
  • Medium: moderate combinations (for example possible with significant impact, likely with moderate impact, unlikely with severe impact). Remediate within 6 months or in the next budget cycle.
  • Low: unlikely or minor combinations. Address as resources allow or accept.

8. Risk treatment

For every finding, management selects one of the following treatments:

  • Mitigate: implement the recommendation or another control to reduce likelihood or impact. An owner and target date are assigned and progress is reviewed at quarterly business reviews.
  • Accept: knowingly accept the risk without further action because the cost of mitigation outweighs the benefit or the risk is within tolerance. Acceptance of critical or high risks must be approved by the executive sponsor, documented with the rationale, and re-evaluated at least annually.
  • Transfer: shift the financial impact to another party, for example through cyber insurance or a contract with a vendor. Transfer does not remove legal responsibility for protecting data.
  • Avoid: stop the activity that creates the risk, for example retiring a legacy system or no longer storing certain data.
