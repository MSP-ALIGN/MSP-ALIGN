Acceptable Use Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

This policy sets the rules for using {{client_name}}'s computers, phones, accounts, networks, email, cloud services, AI tools and data. Its aim is to protect the business, its customers and its staff from security incidents, data loss and legal trouble, while leaving people free to do their jobs.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all employees, contractors, temporary staff, interns and volunteers ("users") who use {{client_name}} systems or information, from any location and on any device, including personal devices used for work under the Mobile Device & BYOD Policy.

3. Roles and responsibilities

  • Management ([name, title]): approves this policy, decides on exceptions and handles violations.
  • Security lead ([name, title]): answers questions about this policy, keeps the list of approved software, cloud services and AI tools current, and collects signed acknowledgments.
  • {{company_name}}: configures and monitors company systems to support this policy and reports suspected misuse to the security lead.
  • Users: follow this policy, protect their accounts and report anything suspicious right away.

4. General use

  • Company systems are provided for business. Limited personal use is allowed when it doesn't interfere with work, use much bandwidth or storage, or put company information at risk.
  • Use company information only for authorized business purposes and only to the extent your job requires.
  • Store company information only in approved company systems. Personal email, personal cloud storage and personal messaging apps are not used for company information.
  • Company data is never stored on or synced to a personal device unless that device is approved and enrolled under the Mobile Device & BYOD Policy.
  • Sign in with your company single sign-on account (for example your Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace account) wherever a service supports it, rather than creating a separate username and password.

5. Accounts and passwords

  • You are responsible for everything done with your account. Never share your password, MFA codes or sign-in session with anyone, including coworkers and managers.
  • Use a unique password of at least 14 characters for each account, kept in the approved password manager.
  • Turn on multi-factor authentication wherever it is available. Never approve an MFA prompt you did not start; report it.
  • {{company_name}} will never ask for your password. Treat any such request as phishing.

6. Email, messaging and internet

  • Be careful with unexpected attachments, links, QR codes and requests for payment, gift cards or credentials, even when they appear to come from someone you know.
  • Verify any request to change payment, banking or payroll details by calling a known phone number, not one in the message.
  • Send Confidential or Restricted information outside the company only through encrypted email or approved secure file sharing, as described in the Data Classification & Handling Policy and the Encryption Policy.
  • Report suspicious messages with the Report button in your email, or by contacting {{company_name}}.

7. Devices and software

  • Lock your screen whenever you step away, and keep company devices with you or locked up when traveling.
  • Do not disable, bypass or uninstall security software, updates, encryption or device management.
  • Install only software, browser extensions and apps that are on the approved list or approved by the security lead. Ask before signing up for any new cloud service for company work, even a free one.
  • Do not connect unknown USB drives or other devices to company computers, and do not connect personal devices to the business network. Use the guest Wi-Fi for personal and visitor devices.
  • Report lost or stolen devices immediately to [security lead name, phone] and {{company_name}} at {{company_emergency_phone}}, so they can be locked or wiped.

8. Artificial intelligence (AI) tools

AI tools can save time, but they can also expose company information or produce wrong answers that look convincing. These rules apply to chat assistants, writing and coding assistants, image and voice generators, and AI features built into other software.

  • Approved tools only. Use only the AI tools approved for company work: [approved tools, e.g. Microsoft 365 Copilot with business data protection]. The security lead keeps this list and adds tools after review.
  • Business accounts only. Use AI tools only through company business accounts whose terms say company data isn't used to train the provider's models. Personal accounts on public AI services (for example personal ChatGPT or Gemini accounts) are not used for company work.
  • Protect sensitive data. Never enter Confidential or Restricted information, such as customer records, protected health information (PHI), Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), card data, Social Security numbers or passwords, into an AI tool that is not approved for that data.
  • Check the output. AI output can be wrong, out of date or made up. The person who uses it is responsible for checking it before it is sent, filed, published or acted on, just as if they had written it themselves.
  • Meeting recorders and note-takers. Do not use AI recording, transcription or note-taking tools in a meeting, online or in person, without telling everyone in the meeting first and getting their agreement where the law requires it, and do not use them at all in meetings where Restricted information is discussed.
  • Report AI-related problems. Treat AI-generated phishing, suspected deepfake calls or video (for example a cloned voice of a manager asking for a payment), and accidentally entering sensitive data into an AI tool as security incidents, and report them right away under section 11.

Training on using AI tools safely is part of the annual security awareness training (see the Security Awareness Training Policy, Required topics).

9. Prohibited activities

Users must not:

  • Access, or try to access, systems, accounts or information they are not authorized to use, or test or probe security controls without written approval.
  • Share credentials or let anyone else use their account.
  • Install or use unapproved software, browser extensions or cloud services for company work ("shadow IT").
  • Copy company or customer information to personal devices, personal accounts or removable media without approval.
  • Use company systems for personal financial gain, outside business ventures, gambling or cryptocurrency mining.
  • Use company systems for political campaigning or fundraising.
  • Send, store or display material that is harassing, discriminatory, threatening, sexually explicit or otherwise offensive.
  • Download, copy or share material in violation of copyright or software licenses.
  • Do anything illegal using company systems.

10. Monitoring and privacy

{{client_name}} and {{company_name}}, acting on its behalf, may monitor and log the use of company systems, networks, email and accounts to protect company information, investigate incidents and comply with the law. Users should have no expectation of privacy in anything they create, store, send or receive on company systems, subject to applicable law.

11. Reporting

Report suspected phishing, lost or stolen devices, unexpected MFA prompts, unusual system behavior or possible data exposure right away to [security lead name, phone] or {{company_name}} at {{company_emergency_phone}}. Do not try to fix the problem yourself. Reporting a mistake quickly is always better than hiding it, and good-faith reports are not punished.

12. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in loss of system access and disciplinary action, up to and including termination of employment or contract. Where a violation involves illegal activity, {{client_name}} may notify law enforcement. Exceptions must be approved in writing by management and recorded by the security lead.
