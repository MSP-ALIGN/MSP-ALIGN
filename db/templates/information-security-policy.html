Information Security Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

This is {{client_name}}'s umbrella information security policy. It states management's commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity and availability of company and customer information, assigns responsibility for the security program, and sets the framework that all other security policies, standards and procedures follow.

The goals of the program are to protect customer, employee and business information from unauthorized access, loss or misuse; keep critical systems available so the business can operate; meet legal, regulatory and contractual obligations; and manage security risk at a level management has knowingly accepted.

2. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All employees, contractors, temporary staff, interns and volunteers of {{client_name}} ("workforce members").
  • All information created, received, stored or transmitted by {{client_name}}, in any format (electronic, paper or verbal).
  • All company-owned or managed devices, servers, networks, cloud services (including Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace) and line-of-business applications, and any personal device used to access company information.
  • Third parties, including {{company_name}} and other vendors, who access or process company information on {{client_name}}'s behalf.

3. Management commitment

The owners and leadership of {{client_name}} (and its board of directors, where it has one) support this program and will:

  • Provide reasonable budget, staff time and outside expertise to implement and maintain security controls.
  • Approve security policies and review the state of the program at least annually. Where bylaws, a parent organization, funders or regulators require it, the board of directors approves the policies.
  • Make risk decisions (accept, mitigate, transfer or avoid) for risks identified in risk assessments, and document those decisions.
  • Set the example by following the same policies as the rest of the workforce, including MFA and training requirements.

4. Roles and responsibilities

  • Board of directors (if required): approves this policy set when it is adopted and when it changes significantly, and receives the annual security report. [Remove this role if {{client_name}} has no board approval requirement.]
  • Executive sponsor ([name, title]): accountable for the program, approves policies and risk decisions (or recommends policies to the board where board approval is required), and receives the annual security report.
  • Security lead ([name, title]): the internal person designated to coordinate the program day to day. Maintains this policy set, tracks remediation items, is the first internal contact for security questions and incidents, and works with {{company_name}}. Where required by regulation, this person is also the HIPAA Security Official or FTC Safeguards Rule Qualified Individual.
  • {{company_name}} (managed service provider): implements and operates technical controls under its service agreement (endpoint protection, patching, backups, monitoring, identity and device management), advises on risk, and provides reporting through {{vcio_name}}, the assigned vCIO. {{client_name}} keeps overall responsibility for its security program.
  • Managers: ensure their staff complete training, request and approve access for their team, and notify the security lead of role changes and departures on time.
  • All workforce members: follow this policy and related policies, protect their credentials, complete required training, and report suspected incidents immediately.

5. Risk management

  • A security risk assessment is performed at least annually and after major changes such as a new line-of-business system, office move, merger or significant incident.
  • Risks are rated using the likelihood and impact scales in the Security Risk Assessment Report and recorded in a risk register with an owner and target date.
  • High and critical risks require a documented treatment decision by the executive sponsor within 30 days of the assessment.
  • Accepted risks are re-reviewed at least annually. Risk acceptance must be in writing and cannot be granted by the person who owns the affected control alone.
  • {{client_name}} maintains cyber liability insurance appropriate to its size and risk, reviewed at renewal against the insurer's control requirements.

6. Policy framework

Security documentation is organized in three levels:

  1. Policies (this document and topic policies) state what must be done and who is responsible. They are approved by the executive sponsor, or by the board of directors where that is required.
  2. Standards define specific settings and technologies, for example the endpoint baseline or approved software list. They are maintained by the security lead and {{company_name}}.
  3. Procedures describe step-by-step how tasks are performed, for example onboarding checklists and restore procedures. They are maintained by whoever performs the task.

Topic policies in this set include Access Control, Password & MFA, Remote Access, Mobile Device & BYOD, Encryption, Vulnerability & Patch Management, Logging & Monitoring, Change Management, Asset Management, Backup & Recovery, Vendor Risk Management, Security Awareness Training, Acceptable Use, Data Classification & Handling, Data Retention & Disposal, Risk Management, Client-Built Software, the Incident Response Plan and the Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Plan, plus the Written Information Security Program where the FTC Safeguards Rule applies. Where a topic policy is stricter than this policy, the topic policy applies.

7. Data classification

All company information is assigned one of four classifications. When in doubt, treat information as the higher classification.

  • Public: approved for release to anyone, such as marketing material, the public website and published price lists. No special handling beyond approval before publishing.
  • Internal: day-to-day business information not meant for the public, such as internal procedures, schedules and general email. Shared only with workforce members and authorized vendors; stored only in company-approved systems.
  • Confidential: information whose disclosure could harm {{client_name}}, its customers or employees, such as customer lists, contracts, financial statements, pricing and HR records. Access limited to those who need it; encrypted in transit and at rest; sent externally only through encrypted email or approved secure file sharing.
  • Restricted: the most sensitive information, where disclosure could trigger legal notification duties or serious harm, such as Social Security numbers, bank and card numbers, protected health information (PHI), Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), tax records, passwords and encryption keys. Access limited to named individuals and reviewed quarterly; never stored on removable media or personal devices unless specifically approved and encrypted; access logged.

Data owners (the manager responsible for a business process) decide the classification of the information their process creates and approve access to it. {{company_name}} can help label and protect information using tools such as Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels and data loss prevention. How each level is stored, shared, sent, printed, kept and disposed of, and the extra rules for specific kinds of data such as PHI, card data and CUI, are in the Data Classification & Handling Policy.

8. Baseline security requirements

At a minimum, {{client_name}} maintains the following controls, detailed in the topic policies:

  • Unique user accounts and multi-factor authentication for email, remote access, cloud services and all administrative access.
  • Managed endpoint protection (EDR) with 24/7 monitoring on all workstations and servers, using behavior-based detection, with anti-malware definitions updated automatically and managed centrally by {{company_name}}.
  • A host firewall turned on and managed on every workstation and server.
  • Secure configuration baselines for workstations, servers, network equipment and cloud services: default passwords changed or default accounts disabled, unneeded services and software removed, and settings based on a recognized benchmark (for example CIS Benchmarks). Baselines are kept by {{company_name}} and reviewed at least annually.
  • DNS or web filtering on all company devices, in the office and away from it, blocking known malicious and unwanted sites.
  • Operating system and application patching within defined timeframes.
  • Full-disk encryption on all laptops and workstations.
  • AutoRun and AutoPlay turned off for USB drives and other removable media.
  • New devices are reviewed and approved by {{company_name}} before they join the business network; unknown devices found on the network are investigated and removed.
  • Wireless networks use the current Wi-Fi Protected Access (WPA) standard; where guest Wi-Fi is offered, visitors and personal devices use it, kept separate from the business network (section 9).
  • Backups following the 3-2-1-1-0 approach, with restore testing at least quarterly.
  • Advanced email filtering and email domain authentication (section 10), plus security awareness training and phishing simulations for all users.
  • Secure configuration of cloud services such as Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace (section 10).
  • Physical protection of offices, equipment and paper records (section 11).
  • Background screening of people before they get access to Restricted information (section 12).
  • Out-of-band verification of payment and banking changes (section 13).
  • An up-to-date inventory of hardware, software, accounts and vendors.
  • A tested incident response plan and business continuity plan.

9. Network and wireless security

  • Firewall: every office's internet connection is protected by a managed firewall with current firmware, default passwords changed, and its management interface not reachable from the internet.
  • Business Wi-Fi: uses the current Wi-Fi Protected Access standard: WPA3, or WPA2 with AES (WPA2-Enterprise where possible) only for devices that can't use WPA3, and moves to newer standards as they replace these. WEP, WPA (TKIP), open networks and WPS (push-button or PIN setup) are not used. A shared passphrase is long and unique, known only to {{company_name}} and the people who need it, and changed when someone who knew it leaves.
  • Guest and public Wi-Fi: offered only if it can be done securely: on a separate network (VLAN or SSID) with no access to business systems, devices on it isolated from each other, and its own passphrase or acceptance page. If that separation isn't in place, guest Wi-Fi is not offered.
  • Working on public Wi-Fi: company devices use public or untrusted networks (hotels, coffee shops, airports) only through the VPN or other approved remote access, or a mobile hotspot, as set in the Remote Access Policy (section 8).
  • Segmentation: the network is divided so that a problem in one part can't easily spread: [servers / user workstations / phones (VoIP) / cameras, printers and other smart devices / payment terminals / guest Wi-Fi] are on separate networks (VLANs), with firewall rules allowing only the traffic each needs. Systems reachable from the internet, if any, sit on their own network segment, separate from internal systems. Where card data is handled, the payment environment is isolated as PCI DSS requires and the segmentation is tested at least annually.
  • DNS filtering: devices use the company's approved DNS servers or DNS filtering service, which blocks known malicious, phishing and newly registered domains; DNS query logs are kept as set in the Logging & Monitoring Policy.
  • Wireless access points: listed in the asset inventory with the reason each is needed; vendor default passwords and keys are changed at install; a check for unknown (rogue) access points is run at least [quarterly].
  • Network equipment: switches and wireless access points are listed in the asset inventory, use current firmware (Vulnerability & Patch Management Policy), and are managed only by {{company_name}} and approved staff.
  • Unknown access points and devices: wireless access points, routers or devices not approved by {{company_name}} may not be connected to the business network; any found are investigated and removed.

10. Email and cloud service security

  • Email filtering: all inbound email passes through advanced filtering that checks links and attachments, blocks dangerous file types and protects against impersonation of owners, finance staff and {{client_name}}'s own domains. Mail flow rules or allow lists that bypass filtering are not used without a documented reason.
  • External sender warnings: email from outside {{client_name}} is tagged so staff can see it came from outside.
  • Email domain authentication: every domain {{client_name}} sends email from has SPF and DKIM set up and a DMARC policy of quarantine or reject, with DMARC reports going to a monitored mailbox. Domains that don't send email publish records saying so.
  • Auto-forwarding: automatic forwarding of company email to outside addresses is blocked.
  • Cloud tenant configuration: {{client_name}}'s cloud services ([Microsoft 365 / Google Workspace / other: name]) follow a documented secure baseline maintained by {{company_name}}: MFA through conditional access, legacy sign-in methods blocked, admin accounts separate from day-to-day accounts, external sharing limited to named people, users unable to approve third-party app access on their own, and guest access reviewed. Secure configuration scores and baseline checks are reviewed at least [quarterly] and open items are tracked.
  • New cloud services: a new cloud service is used for company information only after it is approved under the Vendor & Third-Party Risk Management Policy, configured to this baseline where it has comparable settings, and added to the inventory.

11. Physical and environmental security

  • Facility access: offices are locked outside business hours. Keys, badges and alarm codes are issued only to people who need them, listed in [location of the key and code list], collected or deactivated when someone leaves, and alarm codes changed when someone who knew them leaves.
  • Secure areas: server rooms, network closets and cabinets holding Restricted records are locked, with access limited to [named roles] and {{company_name}}. Network jacks in public areas are disabled or on the guest network.
  • Visitors: visitors, including vendors and maintenance staff, sign in, are identified as visitors, and are escorted in areas where Confidential or Restricted information or equipment is kept. The visitor log is kept for at least [3 months / 1 year]. Maintenance staff without system access are supervised while they work on company equipment.
  • Physical access records: where electronic door locks or cameras are used, access logs and recordings for sensitive areas are kept for at least [90 days].
  • Workstations: screens lock automatically after [15] minutes or less, screens facing public areas are positioned or filtered so visitors can't read them, and printouts with Confidential or Restricted information are collected right away.
  • Environmental protection: servers and network equipment are on battery backup (UPS) sized to shut down safely, protected from heat, water and dust, and kept off the floor where flooding is possible. [Temperature and water alerts are in place in the server room.] Cabling is tidy and protected from damage or tapping.
  • Payment terminals (if used): card readers are listed, checked for tampering or substitution every [week / month], and staff at the counter know how to spot and report tampering.
  • Repairs and changes to locks, doors and alarms that protect secure areas are recorded.

12. Personnel security

  • Screening: before getting access to Restricted information or administrative access, new workforce members are screened as local law allows, with [reference checks / identity verification / criminal background check / credit check for finance roles]. Contractors with similar access are screened by their employer, as required in their contract.
  • Agreements: at hire, workforce members sign the Acceptable Use Policy acknowledgment and, where they handle Confidential or Restricted information, a confidentiality agreement. Security responsibilities that continue after leaving (such as confidentiality) are stated in those agreements.
  • Role changes and departures: managers tell the security lead and {{company_name}} of role changes and departures [in advance / on the day], so access is changed or removed under the Access Control Policy and company equipment is returned.

13. Payment and fraud controls

  • Call-back verification: any request to send a payment, change bank or payroll details, pay a new vendor or buy gift cards is verified by calling the requester at a phone number already on file or looked up independently, never one given in the request. This applies even when the request seems to come from an owner or a known vendor.
  • Two-person approval: payments over [$ amount] and any change to vendor or payroll bank details need approval from a second person who did not set up the payment.
  • Separation of duties: where staffing allows, the person who sets up vendors or payees is not the person who approves payments to them, and bank account activity is reviewed by someone other than the person who makes payments.
  • Staff who can move money get the role-based training in the Security Awareness Training Policy.

14. Workforce responsibilities

  • Read and acknowledge this policy and the Acceptable Use Policy at hire and annually.
  • Use company information only for authorized business purposes.
  • Never share passwords or approve MFA prompts you did not start.
  • Report suspected phishing, lost devices, unusual system behavior or possible data exposure right away to [security lead name, phone] or {{company_name}} at {{company_emergency_phone}}. Reporting a mistake quickly is always better than hiding it.

15. Compliance and legal requirements

{{client_name}} identifies the laws, regulations and contracts that apply to it (for example HIPAA, the FTC Safeguards Rule, PCI DSS, CMMC/DFARS, the California Consumer Privacy Act and California's breach notification law, Civil Code 1798.82) and records them in the risk assessment. This policy and its related documents support those requirements but do not by themselves make {{client_name}} compliant with any of them.

16. Exceptions

Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing to the security lead, describing the business reason, the risk, compensating controls and a proposed end date. Exceptions are approved by the executive sponsor, logged in the risk register, and expire after no more than 12 months unless renewed.

17. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in loss of system access and disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract. Where a violation involves illegal activity, {{client_name}} may notify law enforcement.
