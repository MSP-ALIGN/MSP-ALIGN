Information Security Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

This is {{client_name}}'s umbrella information security policy. It states management's commitment to protecting the confidentiality, integrity and availability of company and customer information, assigns responsibility for the security program, and sets the framework that all other security policies, standards and procedures follow.

The goals of the program are to protect customer, employee and business information from unauthorized access, loss or misuse; keep critical systems available so the business can operate; meet legal, regulatory and contractual obligations; and manage security risk at a level management has knowingly accepted.

2. Scope

This policy applies to:

  • All employees, contractors, temporary staff, interns and volunteers of {{client_name}} ("workforce members").
  • All information created, received, stored or transmitted by {{client_name}}, in any format (electronic, paper or verbal).
  • All company-owned or managed devices, servers, networks, cloud services (including Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace) and line-of-business applications, and any personal device used to access company information.
  • Third parties, including {{company_name}} and other vendors, who access or process company information on {{client_name}}'s behalf.

3. Management commitment

The owners and leadership of {{client_name}} support this program and will:

  • Provide reasonable budget, staff time and outside expertise to implement and maintain security controls.
  • Approve security policies and review the state of the program at least annually.
  • Make risk decisions (accept, mitigate, transfer or avoid) for risks identified in risk assessments, and document those decisions.
  • Set the example by following the same policies as the rest of the workforce, including MFA and training requirements.

4. Roles and responsibilities

  • Executive sponsor ([name, title]): accountable for the program, approves policies and risk decisions, and receives the annual security report.
  • Security lead ([name, title]): the internal person designated to coordinate the program day to day. Maintains this policy set, tracks remediation items, is the first internal contact for security questions and incidents, and works with {{company_name}}. Where required by regulation, this person is also the HIPAA Security Official or FTC Safeguards Rule Qualified Individual.
  • {{company_name}} (managed service provider): implements and operates technical controls under its service agreement (endpoint protection, patching, backups, monitoring, identity and device management), advises on risk, and provides reporting through {{vcio_name}}, the assigned vCIO. {{client_name}} keeps overall responsibility for its security program.
  • Managers: ensure their staff complete training, request and approve access for their team, and notify the security lead of role changes and departures on time.
  • All workforce members: follow this policy and related policies, protect their credentials, complete required training, and report suspected incidents immediately.

5. Risk management

  • A security risk assessment is performed at least annually and after major changes such as a new line-of-business system, office move, merger or significant incident.
  • Risks are rated using the likelihood and impact scales in the Security Risk Assessment Report and recorded in a risk register with an owner and target date.
  • High and critical risks require a documented treatment decision by the executive sponsor within 30 days of the assessment.
  • Accepted risks are re-reviewed at least annually. Risk acceptance must be in writing and cannot be granted by the person who owns the affected control alone.
  • {{client_name}} maintains cyber liability insurance appropriate to its size and risk, reviewed at renewal against the insurer's control requirements.

6. Policy framework

Security documentation is organized in three levels:

  1. Policies (this document and topic policies) state what must be done and who is responsible. They are approved by the executive sponsor.
  2. Standards define specific settings and technologies, for example the endpoint baseline or approved software list. They are maintained by the security lead and {{company_name}}.
  3. Procedures describe step-by-step how tasks are performed, for example onboarding checklists and restore procedures. They are maintained by whoever performs the task.

Topic policies in this set include Access Control, Password & MFA, Remote Access, Mobile Device & BYOD, Encryption, Vulnerability & Patch Management, Logging & Monitoring, Change Management, Asset Management, Backup & Recovery, Vendor Risk Management, Security Awareness Training, Acceptable Use, Data Retention & Disposal, the Incident Response Plan and the Business Continuity & Disaster Recovery Plan. Where a topic policy is stricter than this policy, the topic policy applies.

7. Data classification

All company information is assigned one of four classifications. When in doubt, treat information as the higher classification.

  • Public: approved for release to anyone, such as marketing material, the public website and published price lists. No special handling beyond approval before publishing.
  • Internal: day-to-day business information not meant for the public, such as internal procedures, schedules and general email. Shared only with workforce members and authorized vendors; stored only in company-approved systems.
  • Confidential: information whose disclosure could harm {{client_name}}, its customers or employees, such as customer lists, contracts, financial statements, pricing and HR records. Access limited to those who need it; encrypted in transit and at rest; sent externally only through encrypted email or approved secure file sharing.
  • Restricted: the most sensitive information, where disclosure could trigger legal notification duties or serious harm, such as Social Security numbers, bank and card numbers, protected health information (PHI), Controlled Unclassified Information (CUI), tax records, passwords and encryption keys. Access limited to named individuals and reviewed quarterly; never stored on removable media or personal devices unless specifically approved and encrypted; access logged.

Data owners (the manager responsible for a business process) decide the classification of the information their process creates and approve access to it. {{company_name}} can help label and protect information using tools such as Microsoft Purview sensitivity labels and data loss prevention.

8. Baseline security requirements

At a minimum, {{client_name}} maintains the following controls, detailed in the topic policies:

  • Unique user accounts and multi-factor authentication for email, remote access, cloud services and all administrative access.
  • Managed endpoint protection (EDR) with 24/7 monitoring on all workstations and servers.
  • Operating system and application patching within defined timeframes.
  • Full-disk encryption on all laptops and workstations.
  • Backups following the 3-2-1-1-0 approach, with restore testing at least quarterly.
  • Email filtering, security awareness training and phishing simulations for all users.
  • An up-to-date inventory of hardware, software, accounts and vendors.
  • A tested incident response plan and business continuity plan.

9. Workforce responsibilities

  • Read and acknowledge this policy and the Acceptable Use Policy at hire and annually.
  • Use company information only for authorized business purposes.
  • Never share passwords or approve MFA prompts you did not start.
  • Report suspected phishing, lost devices, unusual system behavior or possible data exposure right away to [security lead name, phone] or {{company_name}} at {{company_phone}}. Reporting a mistake quickly is always better than hiding it.

10. Compliance and legal requirements

{{client_name}} identifies the laws, regulations and contracts that apply to it (for example HIPAA, the FTC Safeguards Rule, PCI DSS, CMMC/DFARS, the California Consumer Privacy Act and California's breach notification law, Civil Code 1798.82) and records them in the risk assessment. This policy and its related documents support those requirements but do not by themselves make {{client_name}} compliant with any of them.

11. Exceptions

Exceptions to any security policy must be requested in writing to the security lead, describing the business reason, the risk, compensating controls and a proposed end date. Exceptions are approved by the executive sponsor, logged in the risk register, and expire after no more than 12 months unless renewed.

12. Enforcement

Violations of this policy may result in loss of system access and disciplinary action up to and including termination of employment or contract. Where a violation involves illegal activity, {{client_name}} may notify law enforcement.
