Data Retention & Disposal Policy

{{client_name}} · Effective {{today}} · Maintained with {{company_name}}

1. Purpose

This policy defines how long {{client_name}} keeps records and how records containing sensitive or customer information are securely destroyed when they are no longer needed. Keeping information longer than necessary increases the harm of a breach and the cost of storing and searching it; destroying it too early can break legal, tax or contract obligations. This policy sets a retention schedule, a way to stop deletion when records are needed for litigation or an investigation (legal holds), and secure disposal methods for paper, electronic media, devices and cloud data.

2. Scope

This policy applies to all records and information {{client_name}} creates, receives or keeps, in any format (paper, electronic, email, chat, voicemail, video), wherever they are stored: on-premises servers and computers, mobile devices, removable media, Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace, line-of-business applications, backups, archives, off-site storage and vendors' systems. It applies to all employees, contractors, vendors and {{company_name}} staff who handle {{client_name}} information.

3. Roles and responsibilities

  • Records owner / security lead ({{field:security_lead}}): owns this policy and the retention schedule, issues and releases legal holds, approves exceptions and early disposal, and confirms that disposal is carried out.
  • Data owners: the managers responsible for each kind of record ([e.g. office manager for customer files, controller for financial records, HR for personnel files]). Confirm the retention period for their records, identify records that are past retention, and approve their disposal.
  • {{company_name}}: configures retention and deletion settings in email, cloud storage and backup systems; sanitizes and disposes of devices and media; obtains certificates of destruction; and keeps disposal records.
  • Legal counsel ([name or firm]): advises on legal retention requirements and on when a legal hold is needed or can be released.
  • Users: keep records in approved company systems (not personal email, personal cloud storage or local desktops), follow the retention schedule, never destroy records under a legal hold, and use the approved disposal methods in this policy.

4. Retention principles

  • Records are kept for the period in the retention schedule (section 5), and then securely disposed of unless a legal hold or an approved exception applies.
  • Where a law, regulation or contract requires a longer period than the schedule, the longer period applies. Where it requires deletion sooner, the shorter period applies.
  • Personal and customer information is kept only as long as needed for the purpose it was collected for, as described in {{client_name}}'s privacy notice.
  • Duplicates and convenience copies (printouts, downloaded copies, email attachments saved locally) are not kept longer than the official record and are disposed of when no longer needed.
  • Records are protected for as long as they are kept against loss, destruction, unauthorized change and unauthorized access, according to their classification in the Data Classification & Handling Policy.
  • Where possible, retention is enforced automatically by system settings (for example Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace retention policies and backup retention settings) rather than by people remembering to delete.

5.1 Customer and client records

6. Legal holds

  • When litigation, a government investigation, an audit or a security incident investigation is reasonably expected, the records owner, with legal counsel, issues a legal hold in writing to the people and systems that hold relevant records.
  • A legal hold overrides this policy: records covered by it must not be deleted, changed or disposed of, even if their retention period has passed, until the hold is released in writing.
  • {{company_name}} suspends automatic deletion for the affected mailboxes, sites, devices and backups (for example with litigation hold or a retention policy in Microsoft 365, or Vault holds in Google Workspace) and keeps devices of the people involved instead of wiping or reissuing them.
  • The records owner keeps a list of active legal holds with the matter, scope, custodians, date issued and date released: [location of legal hold register].
  • When the hold is released, the records return to the normal retention schedule.

7. Disposal of records at the end of retention

  • At least [quarterly / annually], data owners, with {{company_name}}, identify records that have passed their retention period and are not under a legal hold, and approve their disposal.
  • Where cardholder data is stored, {{company_name}} checks at least every three months for stored card data that has passed its retention period, and securely deletes it. If card numbers are found anywhere they should not be (for example in email, spreadsheets or scanned forms), they are reported to the security lead, securely deleted, and handled under the Incident Response Plan.
  • Cloud data: data is deleted from the application and from recycle bins, version history and retained copies according to this schedule. Retention policies in Microsoft 365 or Google Workspace are configured to match the schedule (keeping what is required and deleting what is not): [retention policies configured, or "not yet configured"].
  • Deletion requests: where a privacy law gives people the right to have their personal information deleted, verified requests are honored within the legal deadline, unless an exception applies, and passed on to the service providers and contractors who hold the information. The request and the response are recorded.
  • Vendors: when a vendor contract ends, the vendor returns or securely destroys {{client_name}}'s data and confirms this in writing, as required by the Vendor & Third-Party Risk Management Policy.
  • Disposal is recorded with the record category, date range, method, who approved it and who carried it out.

8. Secure disposal methods

8.1 Paper records

  • Paper containing Confidential or Restricted information is cross-cut shredded on site, or placed in locked shred bins and destroyed by a bonded shredding service that provides a certificate of destruction, so that it cannot be read or reconstructed.
  • Paper containing card data is destroyed when no longer needed so that it cannot be reconstructed.
  • Confidential or Restricted paper is never placed in regular trash or recycling.

8.2 Electronic media and devices

All storage media leaving {{client_name}}'s control or being reused, including computers, servers, hard drives and SSDs, copiers and printers with internal drives, phones and tablets, USB drives, memory cards, network devices with stored configurations, and backup media, is sanitized according to NIST SP 800-88 (Guidelines for Media Sanitization) before disposal, return to a lessor or reuse:

  • Clear: overwriting or a factory reset using standard tools. Acceptable only for devices being reused inside {{client_name}} that did not hold Restricted data.
  • Purge: cryptographic erase (destroying the encryption key of a fully encrypted drive), the drive's built-in secure erase or sanitize commands, or degaussing for magnetic media. Required for devices leaving {{client_name}} for resale, donation or return to a lessor, and for reuse of media that held Restricted data, including ePHI, CUI or card data.
  • Destroy: shredding, disintegration, pulverizing or incineration by a certified provider. Required for failed or damaged drives, media that held Restricted data and cannot be purged and verified, and any media where purging cannot be confirmed.
  • After sanitization, the result is verified (for example by the sanitization tool's report or a spot check) before the device leaves {{client_name}}.
  • Equipment sent out for repair or maintenance has its storage removed, or is sanitized first, if it holds CUI or other Restricted data. Drives under warranty are kept and destroyed rather than returned to the manufacturer where the contract allows ("keep your drive").
  • Leased copiers and multifunction printers have their internal drives wiped or removed before return.
  • Encryption keys and recovery keys for disposed devices are deleted from key escrow after sanitization is confirmed.
  • Electronic waste is disposed of in accordance with applicable state and local e-waste laws and is never placed in regular trash.

8.3 Disposal vendors and certificates of destruction

  • Devices and media are disposed of by {{company_name}} or a certified IT asset disposition (ITAD) or shredding vendor ([vendor name]) with a recognized certification (for example NAID AAA, e-Stewards or R2), under a written agreement that requires confidentiality and secure handling.
  • Media is kept in a locked area until it is picked up, and is tracked from {{client_name}}'s premises to destruction.
  • For every disposal, a certificate of sanitization or destruction is obtained that lists each item's serial number or asset tag (or, for paper, the date and weight or container count), the method used, the date, and the person or company that did it.
  • Certificates are kept for the period in section 5.6, and the asset inventory record is updated to "disposed" with the date, method and certificate reference.

9. Review of retention practices

The records owner and {{company_name}} review this policy and the retention schedule at least annually, and when laws, contracts, systems or types of data change, to confirm periods are still correct and that {{client_name}} is not keeping information longer than it needs to. Findings, such as data kept past its retention period or systems without retention settings, are tracked to completion.

10. Exceptions

Keeping records longer than the schedule, or disposing of them earlier, requires a written request to the records owner with the reason, approved by management and, where legal requirements are involved, legal counsel. Approved exceptions are recorded and reviewed at least annually.

11. Enforcement

Destroying records under a legal hold, keeping Restricted information beyond its retention period without approval, or disposing of records or media outside the methods in this policy may result in disciplinary action up to and including termination, and may expose {{client_name}} to legal penalties.
